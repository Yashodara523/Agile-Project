## 1.1 Background and Problem Statement

Hearing-impaired students are poorly served by mainstream e-learning tools. Lessons depend on audio, videos lack captions or sign-language support, and system alerts are sound-based. This project researches and prototypes a **visual-first learning platform** and tests it with real students and teachers.

## 1.3 Scope

**In scope**
- User accounts with three roles: Student, Teacher, Admin
- Lesson delivery with captioned video, sign-language video and illustrated text lessons
- Teacher tools to upload and manage lessons
- Quizzes with visual (non-audio) feedback
- Student progress tracking and teacher dashboard
- Text-based Q&A between students and teachers
- Responsive web interface, high-contrast and low-bandwidth friendly
- Usability testing with hearing-impaired students and teachers
- Deployment of a hosted prototype, user manual and final research report

**Out of scope**
- Native mobile apps
- Live video classes and real-time video chat
- Automatic sign-language recognition or AI translation
- Payments, certificates and a public course marketplace
- Integration with external learning management systems

## 1.6 Success Criteria

The project is successful if: all Must-have stories are Done; at least 85% task success is reached in usability testing; no open critical or high defects at release; and the final report answers all three research questions.

## 1.7 Assumptions

1. A supervisor and at least one partner teacher or school are available for interviews and testing.
2. All team members are available for the full 8 weeks.
3. Sample lesson content is provided by teachers or created by the team.
4. Free-tier or open-source tools are sufficient for development and hosting.

## 1.8 Constraints

- Fixed duration of 8 weeks, no extension.
- No monetary budget; effort is limited to team capacity.
- Testing with minors, if any, requires consent from parents or guardians and supervisor approval.
- Accessibility must follow WCAG 2.1 AA as the reference standard.
