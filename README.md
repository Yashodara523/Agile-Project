## 1. The Problem

Most e-learning platforms assume students can hear. Audio-first lessons, uncaptioned video and sound-based alerts exclude hearing-impaired learners. HearMe explores how a learning platform can be designed **visual-first** so these students can learn on equal terms, including support for Sri Lankan Sign Language.

## 2. Project Goal

Deliver a working, testable prototype of a visual-first e-learning platform and validate it with students and teachers within 8 weeks.

**Research questions**
1. Which visual and sign-language-supported content formats do hearing-impaired students find most understandable?
2. Can a teacher publish accessible lessons without specialist technical skills?
3. Does visual (non-audio) feedback keep learners engaged during quizzes?

## 3. Delivery Approach

| Sprint | Weeks | Theme | Sprint goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1-2 | Discover and foundation | Understand users, agree requirements, set up architecture, accounts and design system |
| 2 | 3-4 | Core learning experience | Learners can open a lesson with captions and sign-language video; teachers can upload lessons |
| 3 | 5-6 | Assess and engage | Quizzes with visual feedback, progress tracking, teacher dashboard, text Q&A |
| 4 | 7-8 | Validate and release | Usability testing, fixes, performance, deployment, final report and demo |

**Milestones:** Requirements baseline (end Week 2) · Alpha, lessons playable (end Week 4) · Beta, feature complete (end Week 6) · Final demo and report (end Week 8)

## 5. How the Project Was Managed

- **Backlog with the Business Analyst:** user research turned into prioritised, testable user stories.
- **Sprint cadence:** planning, a daily 15-minute stand-up, mid-sprint backlog refinement, review and retrospective every two weeks.
- **Accessibility as a quality gate:** a story was not Done until it passed the accessibility checklist.
- **Risk-driven planning:** a living risk register reviewed weekly.
- **Written and visual-first communication:** captions in meetings, written stand-up notes and decisions recorded the same day.
- **Transparent reporting:** a one-page status report to the supervisor at the end of each sprint.

**Key figures**
- Delivered **124 of 139** planned story points (**89%**) and **23 of 26** stories (**88%**).
- Average velocity: **31 points per sprint**.
- **22 of 25** defects found were fixed during the project (**88%**).
- Three of four sprint goals fully met; Sprint 3 partly met, with scope adjusted using the scope flex plan.
- Scope changes were handled through the backlog and change control in Sprints 2 and 3.

## 7. Lessons Learned

1. Involve hearing-impaired users and teachers from Sprint 1, not just at the end.
2. Treat accessibility acceptance criteria as part of every story, not a separate phase.
3. Keep sprint scope realistic; protect the sprint goal over the story count. Sprint 3 was the heaviest at 40 points and was the one sprint not fully met.
4. Make written, visual communication a team standard.
